Go Ecco Climate Control Master Project Specification Document status: Consolidated baseline Purpose: Define the authoritative requirements for the GECC sales, installation, payment, accounting, commission, reporting, and audit system. 1. Purpose and objectives The system shall provide a controlled back-office workflow for GECC projects from initial customer creation through project completion, payment verification, commission processing, and financial reporting. The system shall: Capture project information once. Use the approved Master Sales Record as the authoritative project record. Prevent silent overwriting of approved information. Generate consistent contracts, invoices, certificates, reports, and accounting records. Maintain a complete audit trail. Support controlled changes, formal adjustments, and historical reconstruction. Provide role-based dashboards and access. Support cash-basis financial reporting with operational accounts-receivable and accounts-payable tracking. 2. Authoritative terminology The following terms shall be used consistently throughout the system and documentation. Term Definition Project A GECC customer engagement represented by one project record. Master Sales Record The authoritative, versioned record containing the commercial, customer, project, scope, pricing, cost, and approval information for a project. Project version A numbered snapshot of the Master Sales Record. Approved versions are retained permanently. Approved version A Master Sales Record version that has passed the required approval process and may be used to generate binding documents. Operational update A controlled post-approval addition, such as equipment serial numbers, materials used, installation notes, or completion evidence. Commercial revision Any change affecting price, scope, contract terms, payment terms, cost allocation, commission, or financial reporting. Commercial revisions require a new Master Sales Record version and approval. Completion certificate The document confirming that installation work has been completed or accepted. Payment verification The recorded confirmation that funds have been received and matched to the project. Published financial period A reporting period that has been finalized and locked against ordinary edits. Adjustment entry A formal, separately logged correction to a published financial period. Contractor An individual or entity providing services to GECC under the approved contractor arrangement. System of record The application database. Spreadsheets, PDFs, emails, and exports are outputs and are not authoritative records. 3. Roles and authority 3.1 Sales Associate May: Create and edit draft customer and project information. Prepare draft Master Sales Records. Upload supporting documents. Request approval. May not: Approve the Master Sales Record. Approve commercial revisions. Verify customer payments. Publish financial periods. 3.2 Sales Manager May: Approve Master Sales Records. Approve commercial revisions. Sign contracts and invoices on behalf of GECC. Approve commissions and operational exceptions within assigned authority. Review project profitability and sales performance. 3.3 Comptroller May: Approve Master Sales Records and commercial revisions. Sign contracts and invoices. Verify payments. Approve financial adjustments. Publish and lock financial periods. Approve commission reports. Authorize accounting and reporting corrections. 3.4 Installation Manager May: Schedule and coordinate installation. Approve installation-related operational costs. Review technician submissions. Sign completion certificates. Act as certificate signer when the authorized substitute rule applies. The Installation Manager does not approve the Master Sales Record unless a later, formally approved policy explicitly grants that authority. 3.5 Technician May: View assigned installation information. Enter serial numbers, materials used, installation notes, photographs, and completion evidence. Submit proposed scope or material changes for review. May not: Change approved commercial terms. Approve their own costs. Mark a project financially complete. 3.6 Accounts Payable Associate May: Prepare payable records for approved equipment, dealer fees, materials, and contractor obligations. Reconcile payable evidence. Submit payable batches for approval. 3.7 Database Administrator May: Maintain system configuration and technical access. Manage backups, integrations, and operational maintenance. Access audit logs only where technically necessary and with a recorded reason. The Database Administrator may not alter business records outside the approved correction process. 4. Project lifecycle A project shall progress through the following controlled states: Draft Submitted for approval Approved Contract and invoice signing Awaiting installation eligibility Installation scheduled Installation in progress Installation complete Completion certificate signed Payment verification pending Payment verified Completed Cancelled Closed A project may not move to Completed until: Required installation work is complete. The completion certificate has been signed. Required payment has been verified. Any required exception or adjustment has been approved. Cancelled project codes may not be reused. Reopening a cancelled or closed project requires authorized approval and creates a new auditable event. 5. Master Sales Record The Master Sales Record shall contain, at minimum: Project identification code. Customer and contact information. Project and installation location. Sales Associate and responsible personnel. Scope of work. Equipment and materials. Pricing, discounts, taxes, and payment terms. Dealer fees and other project costs. Technician and installation costs. Commission and fixed-cost inputs. Required documents. Approval history. Signature status. Installation status. Payment status. Completion status. Revision history. 5.1 Versioning rules Draft records may be edited by authorized users. Approval creates a numbered approved version. Approved versions are immutable. Commercial changes require a new version, approval, and a recorded reason. Operational information may be appended after approval where it does not change the commercial agreement. Operational updates must identify the user, timestamp, affected field, previous value where applicable, and supporting evidence. Generated documents must identify the Master Sales Record version from which they were created. 6. Approval and signature rules The Sales Manager or Comptroller shall approve the Master Sales Record. The Installation Manager may approve installation-related operational costs but does not replace the Sales Manager or Comptroller as commercial approver. The normal signature order is: Customer signs the contract. Customer signs the invoice or payment document. Authorized GECC signer signs the contract and invoice. Installation proceeds only after the waiting-period requirement is satisfied. Installation Manager signs the completion certificate. Customer signs the completion certificate. Payment is verified. The project is marked complete. The permitted GECC signers are the Sales Manager and Comptroller. The Installation Manager signs completion certificates only, except where an approved substitute rule applies. 7. Installation and waiting period Installation may not begin until at least three calendar days have elapsed after the customer has signed both the contract and invoice. The waiting period begins when the later of the two customer signatures is recorded. The system shall: Calculate the earliest permitted installation date. Prevent scheduling before that date unless an authorized exception is recorded. Record the person approving an exception and the reason. Preserve all changes to the installation date. Record installation start and completion timestamps. 8. Costs, compensation, and commissions Project costs shall be categorized consistently as: Equipment. Materials. Dealer fees. Lead costs. Technician costs. Installation costs. Contractor compensation. Other approved project costs. Lead, Damon, and Jerry deductions shall be treated as defined project-level cost allocations. They shall not be entered inconsistently as both universal deductions and discretionary payments. Fixed compensation shall be recorded separately from project cost allocation and shall be payable only when the applicable project reaches the defined completion state. Commissions shall: Be calculated from the approved project version and recorded cost inputs. Not be earned before project completion. Include a calculation snapshot. Identify the recipient, rate, basis, deductions, and final amount. Be included in the appropriate commission report. Be changed only through a formal correction or adjustment. 9. Payments and payables 9.1 Payment verification For cashier’s checks, payment verification shall include confirmation that the check has been accepted or funds have been confirmed through the approved banking process. For bank wires, payment verification shall include: Bank reference number or equivalent proof. Amount. Date received. Receiving account. Project allocation. Verifying user. Payment status shall be one of: Unpaid. Evidence submitted. Verification pending. Verified. Rejected. Reversed. Refunded. Partially paid. Only a Sales Manager or Comptroller may approve a payment reversal, refund, or allocation correction. 9.2 Accounts payable Payable records shall be created for approved equipment, dealer fees, materials, and contractor obligations when the relevant triggering event occurs. Each payable shall include: Supplier or contractor. Project. Category. Amount. Supporting evidence. Approval state. Due date. Payment state. Related accounting entry. 10. Accounting and reporting The system shall maintain an internal double-entry ledger. Reports shall use cash-basis presentation unless a report is explicitly identified as an operational subledger or management report. The system shall separately track: Accounts receivable. Accounts payable. Inventory. Fixed assets. Depreciation. Loans. Revenue. Project costs. Commissions. Cash. Equity and retained earnings. Adjustments. The initial chart of accounts shall be approved before financial reporting is released. Published periods shall be locked. Corrections to published periods shall use adjustment entries rather than direct edits. Required reports include: Statement of operations. Balance sheet. Cash-flow statement. Accounts receivable. Accounts payable. Inventory. Fixed assets and depreciation. Loans. Project profitability. Commission reports. Working-capital summary. Exception and reconciliation reports. 11. Documents and signatures Documents shall be generated from the approved Master Sales Record version and shall include: Project code. Customer and location. Applicable prices and terms. Version identifier. Generation timestamp. Required signatures. Signature status. Supporting audit reference. The system shall support: Signature authentication. Ordered signing. Reminder notices. Signature expiration after 75 days. Signed PDF storage. Signature certificate storage. Signature event audit records. Final document templates, logo assets, state-specific artwork, and exact signature placement shall be treated as controlled configuration inputs. 12. Inventory, assets, and loans The system shall support: FIFO inventory valuation. Equipment serial-number tracking. Materials issued to projects. Returned or unused materials. Vehicles. Computers. Office assets. Straight-line depreciation. Loans and repayment schedules. Asset disposal and transfer records. Inventory and asset records shall be linked to projects and accounting entries where applicable. 13. Audit, security, and retention The system shall use least-privilege access and role-based permissions. The audit trail shall record: Login and access events. Record creation. Record edits. Approvals. Rejections. Version creation. Signature events. Payment verification. Financial adjustments. Period publication and locking. Permission changes. Export and deletion attempts. Audit entries shall be append-only, tamper-evident, and permanently retained. No user, including an administrator, may silently delete or rewrite a business record or audit entry. 14. Dashboard and notifications Role-specific dashboards shall show: Projects requiring action. Pending approvals. Expiring signatures. Installation scheduling issues. Missing completion evidence. Unverified payments. Unpaid receivables. Unapproved payables. Profitability exceptions. Commission items awaiting approval. Financial periods awaiting close. Data-quality and reconciliation exceptions. Notifications shall be generated from system events and shall not replace the underlying record or audit entry. 15. Data migration and quality Before production use, existing spreadsheets and source data shall be migrated through a controlled process: Extract source data. Stage the data without altering the source. Normalize names, addresses, project codes, dates, and categories. Detect duplicates. Resolve conflicts. Map legacy values to controlled terminology. Import approved records. Reconcile totals and record counts. Obtain migration approval. Preserve the original source files and migration log. 16. Implementation sequence The project shall proceed through the following workstreams: Baseline requirements and terminology. Data model and database. User roles and permissions. Audit logging. Customer and project records. Master Sales Record and versioning. Workflow and approvals. Document generation. Electronic signatures. Payments and payment verification. Costs and commissions. Inventory, assets, and loans. Financial reporting. Dashboard and notifications. Testing, migration, and reconciliation. Deployment, training, and operational handover. Each Sprint shall identify: Scope. Dependencies. Inputs. Outputs. Acceptance criteria. Decisions made. Risks and unresolved issues. Requirements superseded by the Sprint. 17. Consolidation decisions applied The following decisions resolve the principal contradictions found in the source bundle: Commercial approval: Sales Manager or Comptroller. Installation approval: Installation Manager approves installation-related operational costs only. Record immutability: Approved commercial versions are immutable; controlled operational updates are appended without silently changing commercial history. Financial model: Cash-basis reporting is retained, with separate operational AR/AP tracking. Historical calculations: Financial calculations used for approvals, commissions, and published reports must be stored as snapshots. Waiting period: Three calendar days, beginning after the later customer signature. Terminology: “Contractor” is the standard workforce term; “payroll” is replaced with “contractor payment processing” unless employees are formally introduced. Corrections: Published financial periods and approved records are corrected through versions or adjustment entries, never direct overwrites. Cross-references: Requirements refer to the numbered sections of this master specification rather than duplicated Sprint-specific wording.
