# VERIDIAN User Guide: Understanding Multi-Stage Workflows

**Documentation status:** Approved business workflow; controlled development in progress.

## What is a multi-stage workflow?

A multi-stage workflow divides a large job into smaller steps called
**stages**. Each stage has tasks, checks, and sometimes approvals.

You complete the required work in one stage before moving forward.

VERIDIAN uses workflows to help employees:
- Follow the correct order.
- Avoid mistakes and missing information.
- Know what work remains.
- Record who completed or approved important actions.
- Keep a reliable history of each project.

**Important:** A completed stage does not automatically approve
the next stage. VERIDIAN must check that the required evidence exists.

## Example: The three-stage Invoice workflow

### Stage 1: Before installation

The required pre-installation checks include:
- A current approved Master Sales Report (MSR).
- A verified customer-signed Contract.
- The required 50% deposit, or a properly approved payment exception.
- The applicable cancellation requirements.
- The required waiting period.

A customer signature on the final Invoice is not a pre-installation
requirement under the newly approved workflow.

**Safety note:** The calculation of the five-day waiting period
must be reviewed and tested before the new workflow is activated.
The existing live safeguards remain in effect during development.

### Stage 2: Identify the equipment

Before installation, the Installation Manager's process must account
for every physical equipment unit.

Record each unit's serial number, or document the specific authorized
exception when a serial number genuinely does not apply.

Serial numbers identify the actual equipment installed at the
customer's property.

Do not change an already approved or signed Invoice merely to add
serial numbers.

### Stage 3: After installation

After the installation is completed and the required completion
checks are satisfied:

- Prepare the final customer Invoice.
- Prepare the versioned equipment-identification addendum.
- Arrange delivery to the correct customer.
- Verify delivery using trustworthy evidence.
- Preserve the historical documents and audit records.

A document marked **prepared**, **sent**, or **observed** is not
automatically proof that the customer received it.

## What happens if a stage is incomplete?

VERIDIAN should identify what is missing and prevent the next
controlled action until the required evidence has been verified.

An authorized exception must be specific, documented, and approved
by the correct person. Exceptions do not remove unrelated safety,
signature, cancellation, or audit requirements.

## What is an audit record?

An audit record helps answer:

1. Who performed the action?
2. What action was performed?
3. Which project and MSR version were involved?
4. When did the action happen?
5. What evidence supports the action?
6. Was an exception or approval required?

Historical approved or signed records must remain preserved.
Corrections are recorded through controlled updates or new versions,
rather than silently changing old records.

## Roles and responsibilities

The Sales team prepares accurate project information and supports
the approved Contract workflow.

The Installation Manager coordinates installation readiness and
equipment identification.

The Comptroller performs financial and other specifically authorized
verification duties.

The CEO handles exceptions only within approved authority.

Each employee must have the proper role or explicitly approved
temporary authority. A user cannot approve their own work when
independent approval is required.

## A simple way to remember the stages

**Check before. Identify during preparation. Document and deliver after.**

The goal is not simply to move a project forward.
The goal is to move it forward correctly, with proof at every step.
